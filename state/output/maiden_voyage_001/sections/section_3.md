## Medical Terminology

The product name is not set, and no scout findings are available. The following is therefore a neutral terminology baseline for the research phase, not a product-specific medical vocabulary. Use plain language alongside clinical terms, spell out abbreviations at first mention, and have clinical, regulatory, and legal reviewers approve any final terminology.

### Core clinical vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Plain-language guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Beginning suddenly or lasting a short time | “A sudden or short-term condition” |
| Chronic | Continuing for a long time or recurring | “A long-lasting or recurring condition” |
| Diagnosis | Identification of a condition based on evidence and evaluation | “What the condition is” |
| Differential diagnosis | A list of possible conditions considered during evaluation | “Other conditions that may explain the findings” |
| Etiology | The cause or origin of a condition | “What caused it” |
| Idiopathic | Having no identified cause | “The cause is unknown” |
| Iatrogenic | Caused by medical care or treatment | “Caused by a healthcare intervention” |
| Prognosis | The likely course or outcome of a condition | “What may happen over time” |
| Symptom | A change perceived by the person | “Something the person experiences” |
| Sign | An objectively observable finding | “Something a clinician can observe or measure” |
| Comorbidity | A condition existing alongside another condition | “Another ongoing health condition” |
| Contraindication | A reason not to use a treatment or intervention | “A situation in which the treatment should not be used” |
| Adverse event | An unwanted medical event occurring after exposure or treatment | “An unwanted health event” |
| Side effect | An unwanted response associated with a treatment | “An additional effect of the treatment” |
| Interaction | An effect caused when two or more substances or treatments act together | “What can happen when they are combined” |
| Titer | A measured concentration of antibodies or other substances | “The amount of a substance in a sample” |
| Placebo | An inactive treatment used for comparison in research | “A treatment with no active study ingredient” |
| Endpoint | A defined outcome used to evaluate a study or treatment | “The result being measured” |

### Anatomical and measurement terms

| Term or abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anterior / posterior | Toward the front / toward the back |
| Superior / inferior | Toward the upper / lower part of the body |
| Medial / lateral | Toward the midline / away from the midline |
| Unilateral / bilateral | Affecting one side / affecting both sides |
| mg | Milligram, one-thousandth of a gram |
| mcg | Microgram, one-millionth of a gram |
| mL | Milliliter, one-thousandth of a liter |
| mmHg | Millimeters of mercury, a unit of pressure |
| bpm | Beats per minute |
| PO | By mouth |
| IV | Intravenously, into a vein |
| IM | Intramuscularly, into a muscle |

Medical terminology should remain precise, understandable, and traceable to authoritative clinical sources throughout the project.
