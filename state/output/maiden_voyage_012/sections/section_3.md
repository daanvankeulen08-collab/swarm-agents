### Abbreviation and Ambiguity Checklist

Never assign meaning to an abbreviation without reviewing the complete source wording and clinical context.

| Source | Possible Meaning | Required Check | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| MS | multiple sclerosis; mitral stenosis | Review patient history, medication, or procedure context | Ask the requesting professional; document the confirmed expansion |
| ER | emergency room; estrogen receptor | Check location and assessment type | Preserve the intended meaning in the target language |
| Hx | history | Confirm whether “medical history” or another history is intended | Expand only after confirmation |
| Rx | prescription | Verify medication name, dose, route, and frequency | Read all medication details exactly as written |
| [Abbreviation] | [Possible expansion(s)] | [Context needed] | [Clarification and documentation step] |

For every assignment:

- [ ] Record the hospital or clinic, department, language pair, and interpretation setting.
- [ ] Identify approved glossaries, terminology databases, and institutional protocols.
- [ ] Flag abbreviations that have multiple meanings.
- [ ] Preserve medication names, doses, routes, frequencies, and units exactly.
- [ ] Confirm anatomical terms, procedure names, and diagnostic labels in context.
- [ ] Ask a qualified healthcare professional when meaning remains unclear.
- [ ] Record the confirmed term, source used, date, and unresolved question.
- [ ] Avoid placing patient names, identifiers, or unnecessary clinical details in personal notes.
- [ ] Secure or delete notes according to hospital and privacy policies.
- [ ] Review pending terminology items before the next related assignment.
