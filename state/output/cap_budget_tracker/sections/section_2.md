## Expense Categories

A consistent category system keeps irregular income understandable, separates business costs from personal spending, and makes tax preparation easier. Categorize each transaction when it is recorded rather than trying to reorganize expenses later.

## Category Structure

| Category | Included expenses | Tracking treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Software & Subscriptions | Project-management tools, design software, cloud storage, business apps | Record recurring charges and renewal dates |
| Home Office | Internet, electricity, dedicated equipment, desk supplies | Track actual costs and any locally permitted home-office allocation |
| Travel & Client Meetings | Public transport, flights, hotels, mileage, meals during meetings | Record destination, client or project, and business purpose |
| Professional Development | Courses, workshops, books, certifications, conferences | Separate required training from optional learning |
| Marketing & Sales | Advertising, portfolio services, print materials, event fees | Track campaign or vendor where possible |
| Professional Services | Accountant, lawyer, bookkeeping, contractor assistance | Record invoice recipient and payment date |
| Insurance & Compliance | Business insurance, registrations, licenses, filing fees | Review policy dates and renewal costs quarterly |
| Taxes & Government | Quarterly tax payments, sales tax, payroll or local charges | Reconcile payments with records; use a separate tax-reserve account |
| Owner Draws & Personal | Personal spending funded through the business | Track separately; do not treat as a business expense |
| Miscellaneous | Costs that do not fit another category | Add a short explanation and recategorize regularly |

Tax treatment should always follow local requirements. A separate tax-reserve transfer helps prevent tax money from being accidentally spent.

## Categorization Checklist

Before submitting an expense:

- [ ] Record the date, amount, vendor, and payment method.
- [ ] Assign exactly one primary category.
- [ ] Add a project, client, or business-purpose note when relevant.
- [ ] Attach a receipt or digital invoice.
- [ ] Confirm the payment method and account used.
- [ ] Separate billable reimbursable costs from overhead.
- [ ] Record owner draws as personal, not business expenses.
- [ ] Check whether the cost may have tax or deductible implications.
- [ ] Move uncertain or duplicate entries to a review list.
- [ ] Correct categories before completing the monthly review.

## Monthly Review Checklist

- [ ] Compare logged expenses with bank and card statements.
- [ ] Investigate missing receipts and uncategorized transactions.
- [ ] Review subscriptions for unused or duplicate services.
- [ ] Separate one-time purchases from recurring costs.
- [ ] Compare actual spending with the current monthly plan.
- [ ] Calculate the average expense total across several months.
- [ ] Confirm that tax-reserve transfers remain intact.
- [ ] Back up receipts and the completed expense log.
- [ ] Export records for an accountant when needed.

## Conclusion

A complete expense system requires consistent categories, complete transaction details, retained receipts, and monthly reconciliation. Reviewing categories regularly gives freelancers a clearer view of true operating costs, taxable expenses, recurring commitments, and personal draws—even when monthly income varies substantially.
