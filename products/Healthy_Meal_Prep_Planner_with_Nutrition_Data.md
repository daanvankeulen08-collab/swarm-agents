## 5. Storage and Safety Checklist

- ☐ Refrigerate perishables promptly; keep the refrigerator at **40°F/4°C or below**.
- ☐ Cool cooked food before loosely closing containers.
- ☐ Label each container with the **meal name and preparation date**.
- ☐ Refrigerate meals for the first **3–4 days** of the week.
- ☐ Freeze later portions at **0°F/-18°C**.
- ☐ Thaw frozen food safely in the refrigerator, cold water, or microwave—not on the counter.
- ☐ Reheat meals to **165°F/74°C** before eating.
- ☐ Keep raw proteins separate from vegetables and ready-to-eat foods.

## Conclusion

A successful prep session depends on a verified meal plan, overlapping ingredients, realistic batch sizes, and dated storage. Completing these checklists creates a varied, nutrition-aware week while reducing last-minute decisions and food waste.

### Measurement and calculation rules

- Weigh the edible portion in the same state listed: raw foods before preparation, cooked foods after cooking, and plain oil by mass.
- Do not substitute raw poultry values for cooked poultry or vice versa without a verified yield conversion.
- Retain USDA display precision in stored data. Round derived meal totals only after summing all ingredients.
- To scale any row to a recipe portion, use:  
  **portion nutrient = per-100-g nutrient × weighed portion in grams ÷ 100**.
- Recipe portions are user-selected weights, not standardized servings. For example, 200 g of roasted chicken provides 330 kcal, 62 g protein, 0 g carbohydrate, 7.14 g fat, 0 g fiber, and 148 mg sodium. This is a portion calculation, not a USDA serving size.
- Never impute a missing value. Use **“Nutrition data not available”** only when the cited source omits that nutrient.

## Storage & Safety

Label every meal component when it is cooked or opened. Each label must include the **dish/component, batch size, cooking or opening date, “refrigerate by” date, “frozen on” date, “freezer use-by” date, portion count/weight, and linked Nutrition Data entry or ID**. When moving food from refrigerator to freezer, retain the original cooking date and add the freezer details—never overwrite or reset dates. Use the earliest applicable date.

**Illustrative label:** `Turkey chili—4 servings | cooked 2025-03-06 | refrigerate by 2025-03-09 | frozen 2025-03-06 | freezer use-by 2025-06-06 | Nutrition Data entry: linked`

### Danger-Zone and Handling Checklist

- [ ] Keep refrigerated food at **40°F/4°C or colder** and frozen food at **0°F/-18°C**. The danger zone is **40–140°F/4–60°C**.
- [ ] Allow no more than **two cumulative hours above 40°F for any food**. This includes unrefrigerated transport, meal staging, and countertop thawing. Above **90°F/32°C**, the cumulative limit is **one hour**. For example, 45 minutes in transit plus 30 minutes thawing leaves only 45 minutes remaining at ordinary temperatures.
- [ ] Record departure, arrival, thawing, and return times. Never restart the clock. Time in an insulated cooler may be excluded only if a thermometer confirms the food remained at or below 40°F.
- [ ] Refrigerate perishables immediately. Spread large portions into shallow containers, cool promptly, cover loosely while cooling, and seal once chilled.
- [ ] Thaw safely in the refrigerator, in cold water changed every 30 minutes, or in the microwave followed by immediate cooking. Refrigerator thawing does not use the danger-zone allowance; record the thaw date and use refrigerated, thawed food within 24–48 hours.
- [ ] Reheat meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, rice, pasta, soups, and reheated leftovers to **165°F/74°C**. Begin reheating promptly; it does not reset the cumulative danger-zone clock.
- [ ] Discard food that exceeded its limit, has an unknown history, or appears unsafe. Smell and appearance cannot establish safety.

### Complete Short-Term and Freezer Guide

*Freezer periods are conservative quality windows for food maintained continuously at 0°F. Follow package dates and local food-safety guidance if they differ.*

| Meal component | Short-term storage | Freezer use-by | Safe handling and realistic example |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Cooked rice, pasta, bulgur, quinoa, couscous, and other grains** | 3–4 days | 2–3 months | Cool promptly; portion into airtight containers. For example, freeze four cups of brown rice as four 1-cup servings. Thaw in refrigerator. |
| Cooked potatoes or sweet potatoes | 3–4 days | 2–3 months | Cool before covering; bake or reheat directly from frozen where safe. |
| Cooked vegetables and mixed vegetable sides | 3–4 days | 8–12 months | Freeze without sauce; thaw in refrigerator. Example: broccoli, carrots, and peppers for weekly bowls. |
| Cut or sliced fruit | 3–4 days | 2–3 months | Use lemon juice to reduce browning where appropriate; freeze in portions. |
| Raw meat, poultry, and seafood | Ground meat, poultry, and shellfish: 1–2 days; steaks/chops/roasts: 3–5 days | Follow cut-specific package date; commonly 3–12 months | Freeze immediately in meal portions. Keep raw seafood leakproof and separate from produce. |
| Cooked meat, poultry, and seafood | 3–4 days | 2–4 months | Freeze in complete portions with sauce or immediately after cooling. Reheat to 165°F. |
| Cooked beans, lentils, tofu, and tempeh | 3–4 days | 2–3 months | Cool cooked beans before freezing; freeze sauce separately when practical. |
| Soups, stews, curries, and chili | 3–4 days | 2–3 months | Cool in shallow containers; freeze only once fully chilled. Thaw in refrigerator and boil or reheat to 165°F. |

| Milk, yogurt, cheese, and plant-based alternatives | Follow the package use-by date after opening | Freeze only when product guidance permits | Freezing may change texture. Keep opened dairy products in the refrigerator and use by their labeled date. |

### Final Meal-Prep Checklist

- [ ] Shop for refrigerated and frozen food last; use an insulated bag and separate raw proteins from ready-to-eat foods.
- [ ] Use sanitized containers, boards, and utensils; never return cooked food to raw-meat equipment.
- [ ] Weigh portions after preparation or reheating and match them to the planner’s Nutrition Data entry.
- [ ] Put food away before leaving the kitchen, then maintain an updated refrigerator/freezer inventory.

**Conclusion:** Consistent labels, continuous cold-chain control, cumulative danger-zone tracking, and component-specific dates make weekly meal preparation safer, easier to audit, and less wasteful.
